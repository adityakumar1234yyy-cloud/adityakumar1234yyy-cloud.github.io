Cyclistic Bike-Share Analysis

Understanding how casual riders and members use bike-share differently to inform membership-conversion opportunities.

Explore the case study +

The question

How do member and casual rider behavior differ, and where could the business focus membership messaging?

The workflow

Validated 4,244,891 records from five quarterly CSVs. Removed 2,362 invalid-duration records and retained 4,242,529 trips. Analyzed rider type, duration, weekday, hour and station patterns.

The finding

Members represented 78.1% of rides. Casual rides averaged 39.3 minutes, compared with 12.8 minutes for members. These differences support distinct customer segments and targeted membership messaging.

Titanic Survival Analysis

From missing-value treatment and exploratory charts to hypothesis testing and a reproducible baseline survival model.

Explore the case study +

Analysis approach

Used median imputation, categorical encoding and family-size feature engineering. Explored survival by gender, ticket class and family size.

Model and evaluation

The final logistic regression used passenger class, sex, age, fare and family size. The Week 4 report records 77.44% test accuracy; the repository preserves the earlier Week 3 model separately.

The original Titanic CSV must be supplied to reproduce the scripts; it is not included in the repository.
